# ATX Civil Writing Guide

This guide defines the professional voice, style, and structural conventions the agent
must reproduce when generating feasibility study sections.  It is derived from close
analysis of **20 actual ATXCivil feasibility studies** produced between 2022 and 2025.

---

## 1. Who ATX Civil Is

ATX Civil is a civil engineering firm serving land developers, investors, and property
owners in the Austin metroplex.  Their feasibility studies are professional engineering
documents — not marketing materials, not legal opinions, not planning applications.
They are reviewed by development attorneys, city/county staff, bank underwriters, and
investors.

The engineer signing the study carries professional responsibility for its accuracy.
The tone reflects that: authoritative, precise, legally grounded, and factual.

---

## 2. Voice and Tone Principles

### 2.1 Third-Person Authoritative

Always refer to the property as "the subject property" or "the site."  Never "your
property" or "this land."

```
✅  The subject property is located within the City of Austin Full Purpose jurisdiction.
❌  Your property is in Austin's Full Purpose jurisdiction.
```

### 2.2 Present Tense for Current Conditions

State current regulatory and physical conditions in present tense.

```
✅  The subject property is designated Zone AE on FIRM panel 48453C0115J.
✅  Wastewater service is available via an 8-inch gravity main located along Wonder Drive.
❌  The subject property was designated Zone AE.
```

### 2.3 Future Tense for Required Actions

Regulatory actions and engineer recommendations use future tense or conditional.

```
✅  A fully-developed floodplain study will be required prior to site plan approval.
✅  A fire flow test will need to be ordered.
✅  Platting will be required prior to the issuance of a building permit.
```

### 2.4 Inline Statutory Citations — Not Footnotes

Every regulatory value cites its governing rule directly in the sentence.  Never use
footnotes or endnotes.  The citation format is:
- City codes: `per City of Austin LDC §25-8-261`
- TAC rules: `per 30 TAC §213.21`
- County codes: `per Travis County Code §482.216`
- IFC: `per IFC [year] §503.2.1`
- LGC: `per Texas LGC §212.004(a)`

```
✅  The maximum impervious cover for this suburban watershed is 60% per CoA LDC §25-8-364.
✅  Fire access roads must maintain a minimum width of 25 feet and 14 feet of vertical
    clearance per IFC 2021 §503.2.1.
❌  The IC limit is 60%.  (see footnote 3)
```

### 2.5 Precise Numeric Values — Always With Units

```
✅  The existing right-of-way is 70 feet.
✅  The subject property is approximately 3.47 acres per TravisCAD.
✅  A minimum setback of 50 feet from the centerline of the minor waterway is required.
❌  The ROW is "about 70 ft wide."
```

### 2.6 Data Source Attribution Within Text

When a value comes from a specific agency record, name it.

```
✅  Per TravisCAD (Account No. 165428), the current improvement value is $485,000.
✅  The FIRM panel number is 48453C0115J, effective January 22, 2020, per the FEMA MSC.
✅  Per the TCEQ Edwards Aquifer viewer, the subject property is located within the
    Edwards Aquifer Contributing Zone.
```

### 2.7 Engineering Precision Over Approximation

Use `±` when a measurement is approximate.  Use `approximately` only when a formal
measurement is not available.

```
✅  The nearest wastewater main is located approximately ±1,137 feet from the site.
✅  Elevations range from approximately 552 to 611 feet above mean sea level.
❌  The WW line is pretty far away — about a mile.
```

## 3. Standard ATXCivil Phrases

These phrases appear verbatim across multiple studies and define the ATX Civil house
style.  Use them when the regulatory situation matches.

### 3.1 No Flood Study Required (Zone X)
> "Based on a review of the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) for [County] County,
> the subject property is located in Zone X (unshaded), indicating the area is outside
> the 500-year floodplain.  No flood study is required.  A pre-submittal meeting with
> [jurisdiction] staff acts as a waiver of the floodplain study requirement."

### 3.2 Zone AE — Atlas 14 FDP Required
> "The subject property contains areas within the 100-year (Zone AE) floodplain as
> shown on FIRM panel [panel_id], effective [date].  A fully-developed floodplain (FDP)
> study using NOAA Atlas 14 Vol. 11 Version 2.0 rainfall data will be required prior to
> site plan approval.  Finished floor elevations shall be set a minimum of 2 feet above
> the base flood elevation per CoA LDC §25-7-92."

### 3.3 OSSF — Wastewater Not Available
> "City/public wastewater service is not available to the subject property.  The nearest
> public main is located approximately [distance] feet from the site, and a Service
> Extension Request (SER) is not recommended.  On-site sewage facilities (OSSF) will be
> required.  The minimum lot size for an advanced OSSF system is 1 acre per 30 TAC
> §285.91.  At [acreage] acres, the subject property [meets / does not meet] this
> minimum."

### 3.4 Fire Flow Test Pending
> "A fire flow test will need to be ordered through [ESD name/number] prior to site plan
> approval.  Per IFC [year] Appendix B, the minimum required fire flow for [building type]
> construction is [value] gallons per minute at [psi] psi residual.  Fire hydrant spacing
> shall not exceed 400 feet from exterior walls, with a second hydrant within 500 feet,
> per IFC [year] §503.2.1."

### 3.5 No Development Agreements Found
> "No development agreements or title commitments were found as public records at the
> time of this report.  A title commitment is recommended to identify any agreements,
> easements, or restrictive covenants that may affect development of the subject property."

### 3.6 CAD Discrepancy — No Material Discrepancy
> "A review of [County] CAD records indicates the subject property is [acreage] acres.
> No material discrepancies were noted between the CAD records and the legal description
> provided."

### 3.7 Plat Exemption
> "Per Texas LGC §212.004(a), a plat exemption may be available for tracts greater than
> [5/10] acres that have access to a public road and do not require public improvements.
> A formal determination from [jurisdiction] Development Services is recommended prior
> to any subdivision activity."

### 3.8 Edwards Aquifer Contributing Zone
> "Per the TCEQ Edwards Aquifer viewer, the subject property is located within the
> Edwards Aquifer Contributing Zone.  Development activity is a regulated activity under
> 30 TAC Chapter 213.  A Water Pollution Abatement Plan (WPAP) will be required prior
> to the issuance of a subdivision plat or site plan permit."

### 3.9 Edwards Aquifer Outside
> "Per the TCEQ Edwards Aquifer viewer, the subject property is located outside the
> Edwards Aquifer Recharge, Contributing, and Transition Zones.  No TCEQ Edwards Aquifer
> permit is required."

### 3.10 Compatibility Standards (CoA)
> "The subject property is adjacent to [single-family / multifamily / etc.] land uses.
> Compatibility standards per CoA LDC Subchapter C will apply to the proposed development,
> including height step-backs and screening requirements.  A detailed compatibility
> analysis will be required at site plan review."

### 3.11 Waterway Setback — Minor Waterway
> "A Critical Water Quality Zone (CWQZ) has been identified on or adjacent to the subject
> property.  The [waterway name] has a contributing drainage area of approximately
> [acreage] acres and is classified as a [minor/intermediate/major] waterway.  A minimum
> [50/100/200]-foot setback from the centerline of the waterway is required per CoA LDC
> §25-8-261 and the Ordinance of November 14, 2019."

## 4. Data Presentation Style

### 4.1 Tables for Comparative or Multi-Value Data

Use a simple two-column table for properties with one zoning district:

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Zoning Code | CS-MU-V-NP |
| Description | General Commercial Services — Mixed Use — Vertical — Neighborhood Plan |
| Maximum Height | 40 ft (compatibility-limited) |
| Maximum Impervious Cover | 80% (zoning); 60% (suburban watershed) |
| Controlling IC Regulation | Suburban watershed (more restrictive) |

Use a four-column table for soils:

| Map Unit | Symbol | Coverage | Hydrologic Group |
|----------|--------|----------|-----------------|
| Austin silty clay | TcA | 57.80% | C |
| Crawford clay | CrD | 42.20% | D |

### 4.2 Bullet Lists for Required Actions

When listing required permits, approvals, or contacts, use a bulleted list — not a
prose paragraph.

```
The following permits and approvals will be required:
• Minor Subdivision Final Plat — City of Austin Development Services
• Site Plan — City of Austin Development Services
• FEMA Fully-Developed Floodplain Study — must be accepted prior to SP submittal
• Fire Flow Test — Austin Fire Department
• Water/Wastewater SER — Austin Water
```

### 4.3 Narrative for Context and Regulatory Interpretation

After any table of data, add a brief narrative paragraph interpreting the significance
for this specific property.  Don't leave tables bare without explanation.

## 5. Hedge Phrases for Uncertain or Pending Data

When a value is not confirmed, use these standard phrases rather than asserting
a value that may be wrong.

| Situation | ATX Civil Phrase |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Fire flow test not yet done | "A fire flow test will need to be ordered [through ESD#X] prior to site plan approval." |
| Utility capacity unknown | "Utility capacity should be confirmed with [provider name] during pre-submittal." |
| Title commitment not reviewed | "A title commitment is recommended to identify any agreements, easements, or restrictive covenants." |
| Survey not provided | "A current boundary survey has not been provided.  Acreage is per [CAD source]." |
| Development agreement search | "A search of public records did not identify any development agreements at the time of this report." |
| OSSF soil evaluation pending | "An OSSF soil evaluation (perc test) will be required to confirm suitability." |
| ERI not yet determined | "An Erosion and Sedimentation Control Assessment (ERI) may be required; final determination will be made by CoA Watershed Protection at pre-submittal." |

**Never** use phrases like "we believe," "we think," "it appears," or "probably."
Engineering documents are assertive. If something is unknown, say it is unknown and
state what action is required.

## 6. Infeasibility Language

When the data reveals a project is infeasible or has a severe constraint, be direct.
Do not soften the finding.

```
✅  The minimum lot size for an advanced on-site sewage facility (OSSF) system is 1 acre
    per 30 TAC §285.91.  At 0.48 acres, the subject property does not meet this minimum
    lot size requirement.  On-site sewage treatment is not feasible for subdivision of
    this property without connection to public wastewater.

❌  The lot size may present some challenges for OSSF if the client decides to subdivide.
```

Follow every infeasibility finding with the two required elements:
1. The specific rule that creates the constraint (with citation)
2. The specific value that fails the test (measured vs. required)

Add a note on whether there is a path forward:
- "De-annexation from the City of Austin ETJ would remove this constraint."
- "A Service Extension Request (SER) to Austin Water is the alternative."
- "No pathway to on-site sewage for this lot size is available under current regulations."

## 7. Jurisdiction-Specific Voice Variations

The fundamental voice is the same across jurisdictions.  These are the variations:

### City of Austin Full Purpose (baseline)
- ASMP road classifications (Level 1–5) not TxDOT functional classes
- LDC citations dominate; reference specific subchapters
- Atlas 14 FDP is the standard flood study method
- IFC 2021 or 2024 depending on project type
- Use "CoA DSD" for permit contacts (not "City Planning")

### Travis County Unincorporated (post-de-annexation)
- "No zoning applies to the subject property.  Travis County is a non-zoning county."
- IC limits from Travis County Code §482.216 (45% commercial; 30% suburban avg lot < 1 ac)
- "LCRA BMP Maintenance Permit is required per Travis County Code §482.944" if Lake Travis watershed
- ESD number required in fire protection section
- OSSF via Travis County Code Ch. 48 (not TCEQ directly, though TCEQ rules apply)
- Atlas 14 FDP or FEMA 500-yr, whichever is greater (Travis County)

### City of Cedar Park / Williamson County
- "The City of Cedar Park adopts the City of Austin Drainage Criteria Manual by reference per Cedar Park Code §12.16.005."
- Williamson County FIRM panels (not Travis)
- ESD#11 (Williamson County ESD) typically covers Cedar Park ETJ
- IFC 2018 unless updated

### City of Round Rock
- IFC 2015 (older edition — always cite the edition)
- RSMP: "The City of Round Rock's Regional Storm Management Program (RSMP) charges $800 per impervious acre, which waives onsite detention requirements for eligible projects."
- Use WCAD (not TCAD) for parcel data
- Atlas 14 rainfall data applied using "centroid rule for Brushy Creek watershed" per Round Rock standard

### City of Kyle ETJ / Hays County
- "The subject property is located within the City of Kyle's Extraterritorial Jurisdiction (ETJ)."
- Rural Subdivision Code applies: min lot width 130 ft, per Kyle Code §41-82(e)
- Hays County FIRM panels
- OSSF via Hays County; "minimum lot size of 1 acre for advanced OSSF per 30 TAC §285.91 is controlling"
- Plum Creek Conservation District note if in Plum Creek watershed

### Dripping Springs / Hays County Unincorporated
- Edwards Contributing Zone → "A Contributing Zone Plan (CZP) must be submitted to TCEQ under 30 TAC §213.21 prior to any permit."
- WTCPUA water service (not Austin Water)
- 2021 Dripping Springs TMP for road classifications
- IFC 2018

### Burnet County (outside Austin metro)
- No zoning
- NFPA 1142 (rural fire water supply, not IFC Appendix B): "A minimum 5,000-gallon on-site water storage tank for fire suppression may be required per NFPA 1142."
- IFC 2024
- NWI wetlands check is required
- Central Texas Groundwater Conservation District note if >25,000 GPD

## 8. Structural Rules

### 8.1 Section Opening
Every section opens with a sentence that states what will be addressed and immediately
establishes the governing authority.

```
✅  3.1 ZONING
    The subject property is zoned CS-MU-V-NP (General Commercial Services — Mixed Use —
    Vertical — Neighborhood Plan) per the City of Austin Land Development Code.
```

### 8.2 Data Then Interpretation
Present the data fact first, then interpret its significance for development.

```
✅  The 100-year floodplain encompasses approximately 0.4 acres in the northern corner
    of the site.  A fully-developed floodplain study will be required, and finished
    floor elevations for any structures within the regulated floodplain must be set
    a minimum of 2 feet above the base flood elevation.

❌  A flood study will be required because the northern corner is in Zone AE.
    (The data fact — "0.4 acres" — is missing.)
```

### 8.3 Every Section Ends With Required Actions

Close each section with a brief forward-looking statement of what the developer must do.
If nothing is required, close with "No action is required for this section at this time."

### 8.4 Summary Section (§4.0)

The summary is a numbered list of key findings and recommendations — typically 3–7 items.
Each item is one sentence.  The tone is crisp, decision-support oriented.

```
4.0 SUMMARY

1. The subject property is zoned CS-MU-V-NP; no rezoning is required for the proposed
   commercial development.
2. The maximum impervious cover is 60% per the suburban watershed classification, which
   governs over the 80% zoning-based limit.
3. A fully-developed floodplain study is required for the Zone AE area in the northern
   corner of the site.
4. A fire flow test must be ordered through Austin Fire Department prior to site plan
   submittal.
5. No development agreements were identified in public records; a title commitment is
   recommended.
```

## 9. What the Agent Must Never Do

| Prohibited | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| Assert a regulatory value without citing the governing rule | Engineer's professional responsibility — every number must be traceable |
| Use "we recommend" or "we suggest" | Feasibility studies state facts and regulatory requirements; they don't give opinions |
| Quote a statute from training data without fetching it from a live source | Code changes — always fetch from MuniCode/TAC/EncodePlus in real time |
| Say "the property is compliant" or "this is allowed" | Legal determination; outside scope of a feasibility study |
| Omit an infeasibility signal to make the study look favorable | Professionally and legally inappropriate |
| Use casual language ("pretty far away," "should be fine") | The engineer stamps this document |
| Generate any section without data facts from the data lake or live tools | Hallucinated values are dangerous; every paragraph needs a data source |
